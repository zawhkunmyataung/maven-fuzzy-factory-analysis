# Maven Fuzzy Factory – E-commerce & Marketing Analysis

## 📊 Business Overview

This project analyses e-commerce business performance across website sessions, orders, products, revenue, costs, refunds, and marketing channels.

The analysis focuses on understanding how website traffic converts into orders, which products contribute most to revenue, which marketing channels generate the strongest business results, and how refunds and campaign performance vary across the business.

The objective is to identify key performance patterns and translate them into actionable business insights.

---

## 🎯 Business Questions

The analysis addresses the following business questions:

- How are orders, revenue, costs, and refunds changing over time?
- Which products generate the highest order volume and net revenue?
- Which marketing channels generate the most orders and revenue?
- Which marketing channels have the highest session conversion rates?
- How does session conversion rate change over time?
- How does refund performance vary across products and marketing channels?
- Which campaign groups contribute most to net revenue?
- Where are there opportunities for further investigation?
- ---

## 🔍 Key Business Findings

### 1. Overall Business Performance

- Total revenue reached approximately **$1.94M**, supported by **32.31K orders**.
- Total COGS was approximately **$722.37K**, while total refunds were **$85.34K**.
- Compared with the previous period, orders increased by **20.15%**, revenue by **21.30%**, COGS by **20.81%**, and refunds by **15.20%**.
- Revenue growth was slightly higher than COGS growth, indicating an improvement in the relationship between revenue generation and product costs.

### 2. Product Performance

- **The Original Mr. Fuzzy** generated the highest order volume at approximately **24.23K orders**.
- It also generated the highest net revenue at approximately **$1.15M**.
- Other major products included **The Forever Love Bear**, **The Birthday Sugar Panda**, and **The Hudson River Mini Bear**.
- The Original Mr. Fuzzy also accounted for the largest refund amount at approximately **$61.84K**, highlighting the importance of monitoring refund performance for high-volume products.

### 3. Refund Performance

- The annual refund rate declined from **6.88% in 2012** to **3.31% in 2015**.
- The decline indicates an overall reduction in the proportion of orders associated with refunds over the period.
- Refund rates across marketing channels were relatively close, ranging from approximately **4.08% to 4.64%**.
- ### 4. Marketing Channel Performance

- **Paid Search** generated the highest order volume at approximately **25.85K orders** and the highest net revenue at approximately **$1.48M**.
- **Organic Search** generated approximately **3.26K orders**, while **Direct** generated approximately **2.86K orders**.
- **Paid Social** generated approximately **0.34K orders**, representing a smaller contribution to overall order volume.
- Marketing channels show different performance patterns depending on whether the metric is order volume, revenue, or conversion efficiency.

### 5. Session Conversion Performance

- **Organic Search** recorded the highest session conversion rate at approximately **7.51%**.
- **Direct** followed at **7.15%**, while **Paid Search** recorded **6.82%**.
- **Paid Social** had the lowest session conversion rate at approximately **3.21%**.
- Overall session conversion rate increased from **4.14% in 2012** to **8.44% in 2015**, indicating an improvement in the conversion of website sessions into orders over time.

### 6. Campaign Performance

- The **Brand** campaign group contributed approximately **$1.48M**, representing around **79.66% of net revenue**.
- **No Campaign** contributed approximately **$0.36M**, or around **19.2%** of net revenue.
- **Desktop Targeted** contributed approximately **$0.02M**, while the **Pilot** campaign group contributed a very small amount.
- Net revenue is therefore highly concentrated in the Brand campaign group, making campaign-level performance an important area for further analysis.

## 💡 Business Implications

### Product Concentration

A large share of orders and net revenue comes from **The Original Mr. Fuzzy**. This concentration creates an opportunity to further investigate product-level demand, customer preferences, inventory planning, and refund patterns.

### Marketing Channel Efficiency

Paid Search contributes the largest share of orders and net revenue, while Organic Search records a higher session conversion rate. This suggests that marketing performance should be evaluated using multiple metrics rather than relying on order volume or revenue alone.

### Conversion Improvement

Overall session conversion increased from **4.14% in 2012 to 8.44% in 2015**. Further analysis could investigate which traffic sources, campaigns, products, or customer segments contributed to this improvement.

### Refund Management

The overall refund rate declined from **6.88% in 2012 to 3.31% in 2015**. Monitoring refund trends by product and marketing channel can help identify potential areas for product or customer-experience improvement.

### Campaign Concentration

The Brand campaign group accounts for approximately **79.66% of net revenue**. This concentration highlights the importance of understanding campaign-level performance and customer acquisition patterns.

## 📌 Conclusion

This analysis provides a business-focused view of Maven Fuzzy Factory's e-commerce performance across products, marketing channels, campaigns, revenue, costs, and refunds.

The analysis highlights strong revenue and order growth, significant product concentration, differences between marketing channel volume and conversion efficiency, and an overall decline in refund rates over time.

The findings can support further investigation into product performance, marketing efficiency, campaign contribution, customer conversion, and refund management.
